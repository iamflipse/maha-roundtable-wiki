MAHA Roundtable — American Health & Nutrition: A Second Opinion

The US Health Care Crisis

Source: opening statements of Sen. Ron Johnson & RFK Jr. in the transcript

The numbers (RFK Jr.)

  • US health-care spend: $4.5 trillion (2022) ≈ $113,000/person — yet Americans live ~7.5 years less than Italians, who spend a fraction of that.
  • 74% of Americans are overweight or obese; 50% of children; 120 years ago obesity was a circus novelty.
  • ~2 in 3 American adults/children have a chronic condition; ~3.8 chronic diseases per average US death.
  • 77% of young Americans are too disabled to serve in the military; early-onset cancers up 79%; 1 in 8 women with breast cancer; 40% of teens with a mental-health diagnosis.
  • $4.5T > military budget; Alzheimer's costs exceed the military budget.

The incentive problem

  • The most valuable asset in the US system is a sick child: pharma, hospitals, and medical schools profit from interventions, not cures.
  • 75% of FDA funding comes from pharma; 80% of NIH grants go to researchers with conflicts of interest (who can collect ~$150K/yr in royalties).
  • SNAP/food stamps: ~70% goes to processed food; Medicaid absorbs the chronic-disease costs.
  • OIC (obesity drugs, Novo Nordisk): 100+ members of Congress support Medicare funding at $1,500/month while being funded by the Danish company; at 74% obesity the tab is ~$3T/yr — vs. the same money buying organic meals + JY (Jordan Peterson?) membership for every obese American (RFK's comparison).
  • ObamaCare's 15% premium rule "incentivizes" insurers to grow the pie.
